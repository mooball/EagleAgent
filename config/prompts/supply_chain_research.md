# Skill Definition: Supply Chain Research

**Supply Chain Mapping Template (v1.0)**

The user wants to research the supply chain for a specific product. Follow this process carefully:

## Step 1: Identify the Product
Before researching the supply chain, you MUST positively identify the product. Search the web to confirm the part number exists (or find the corrected syntax) and confirm the brand/manufacturer.

**Never guess.** If you cannot identify the product with certainty, ask the user for more information — name, brand, description, or application — to help narrow it down.

## Step 2: Map the Supply Chain by Tier
Once the product is positively identified, search the web to find suppliers across the four supply chain tiers below. Aim to find around 5 suppliers per tier, but prioritise quality over quantity — do not pad a tier with poor matches. A tier or sub-category may be empty if no credible suppliers exist.

### Tier A — Manufacturers (The Makers)
- **OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer)** — The brand owner who designs and manufactures the product. Sells only "Genuine" parts. Identify the parent company and manufacturing locations.
- **Aftermarket Manufacturer** — Third-party makers of compatible or equivalent parts ("to fit" other brands). Include quality comparisons where available (e.g. ISO certification, PMA approval).

### Tier B — Industrial Trade Partners (B2B)
- **Trade Wholesaler** — High-volume stockists that carry many brands. Typically require a trade account or login to view pricing.
- **Authorized Dealer** — Third-party businesses with a direct OEM contract. Regional focus, uses the OEM's branding heavily.

### Tier C — General Commercial Sellers (Public Access)
- **Retail / Trade Outlet** — Physical stores with a trade desk that sell to anyone (e.g. Bunnings, Grainger). Visible "Add to Cart" pricing.
- **Online Distributor** — Digital-first platforms (e.g. RS Components, PartSouq) with visible fixed pricing and broad range.

### Tier D — Specialist Commercial (if any exist)
- **Service Exchange (SX) Provider** — Specialises in refurbished/rebuilt heavy components on an exchange basis ("Core Charge", "Reman").
- **Sourcing Broker** — Does not hold stock. Acts as an intermediary offering procurement or global sourcing services.

For each supplier found, state its **name, category, tier, and URL**.

## Step 3: Sourcing Analysis
1. **Geographic Sourcing** — Key sourcing regions (e.g. China, USA, Europe) and typical lead times.
2. **Pricing Landscape** — Price ranges across OEM, aftermarket, and different suppliers to identify cost-effective sourcing options.
3. **Supply Risks** — Any known supply chain risks, shortages, or disruptions affecting this product or category.

Cite sources for all information.
